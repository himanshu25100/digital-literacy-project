# Ransomware

### What the crime is?
Ransomware is a type of malicious software (malware) that encrypts a victim’s files or locks them out of their device, demanding payment—usually in cryptocurrency—to restore access. It is a form of digital extortion that can paralyze organizations, with attackers often stealing data before encrypting it to threaten leakage.

### How a Ransomware Attack Works
1) Infection: Attackers gain access via phishing emails, malicious links, or exploiting software vulnerabilities.
2) Encryption & Execution: The ransomware encrypts files (documents, images, databases) using strong encryption, making them inaccessible. It may also delete backups to prevent recovery.
3) Ransom Note: A message appears demanding payment in exchange for the decryption key.
4) Extortion: Attackers often use "double extortion," threatening to publish sensitive data if the ransom is not paid

### Who is Targeted
Attackers frequently target college students during high-stress periods like "submission week" or final exams. They know students often lack professional-grade security software and are desperate enough to pay to save years of academic work.
For large corporations attack such as that of SnowFlake in 2024, primary targets were enterprise employees and contractors who used "single-factor" authentication (only a password). By targeting just one person with a weak security setup, hackers gained access to the data of millions of customers.

### The Consequences
In case of students, primary consequences can be loss of academic data, which can lead to failure in  grades and delayed graduation.
While in the case of the Snowflake Ransomware attack of 2024, The impact was catastrophic, AT&T lost metadata for nearly all its customers which was apprximately 50 Billion records, and Ticketmaster saw 560 million records compromised. Beyond the multi-million dollar ransoms (AT&T reportedly paid $370,000 to have data deleted), the breach led to a wave of lawsuits and a permanent loss of consumer trust in cloud security.

### Impact and Prevention
Ransomware attacks can lead to significant financial loss, operational downtime, and reputational damage. IBM notes that ransom payments are often seven-figure amounts. 
Some ways for prevention: Maintain regular, offline backups.
Defense Strategies: Use security software, keep systems updated, and train employees on phishing awareness.
