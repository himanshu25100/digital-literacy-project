# Stay Safe Online Checklist

### Financial & UPI Safety
• Verify the 'Payee' name before hitting send: Scammers often use names similar to official brands or friends. Always double-check the verified name that pops up in your UPI app (like BHIM, GPay, or PhonePe) before authorizing any transaction.
• One of the most common UPI scam is to convince people to enter pin to recieve money. Remember, a PIN is only required to send money or check your balance. If someone asks you to "scan and enter PIN" to get a refund or prize, it’s a trap.

### Account & Password Security
• Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA): Use 2FA on your university portals, email, and social media. Even if someone steals your password, they won't get far without that secondary code.

• Use a Passphrase, not a Password: Instead of Password123, use a string of random words or a sentence like ChaiPeCharcha@2026!. It’s harder for bots to crack but easier for you to remember.

### Report Cyber Crime
• If you or a friend have been targeted by a scam or a digital threat, don't hesitate to report it immediately. Speed is especially critical for recovering lost funds.
1) National Helpline: Call 1930 (Available 24/7)
2) Official Portal: cybercrime.gov.in

### Browsing & Connectivity
• Avoid 'Free' Public Wi-Fi for Sensitive Tasks: That coffee shop or railway station Wi-Fi is convenient, but don't use it for banking or logging into student portals. Use your mobile data or a trusted VPN if you must connect.

• Check the URL for Phishing: Before entering credentials on a site (especially for exam registrations or scholarship forms), ensure the URL is correct and starts with https://. Look for the "lock" icon in the address bar.

### Incoming Messages
• Be Skeptical of "Job" or "Scholarship" DMs: If you receive an unsolicited WhatsApp message or DM offering a high-paying part-time job or an "instant" scholarship that requires an upfront registration fee, it is likely a scam.
